## Likely questions and short answers

- **Where does the data come from?** NASA MERRA-2 (v5.12.4): monthly from 1980 to today, ~55 km squares, 9 variables.
- **Why the 1991–2020 normal?** It is the World Meteorological Organization's current standard reference period.
- **What does "clear change" mean?** p < 0.01, the 95% interval doesn't include zero, and three separate checks point the same way. If an independent source (station, satellite) disagrees, "clear" is not given.
- **Doesn't testing many places at once produce false alarms?** The map has a multiple testing correction (FDR); on artificial data with no trend the false alarm rate is below 5.5%.
- **Why compute in the browser?** Changing the period gives an instant answer, and thousands of people can use it at once. For the API, the same code runs on a Vercel server.
- **How accurate is it locally?** A 55 km grid; it represents a region, not a single village. We say this clearly on the site.
- **Can other apps use it?** Yes, there's an open API: `/api/v1/trend?place=TR-21&var=T2M` (on the site: ⓘ → API).

## Method questions (verified from the code: `web/src/trend.ts`, `worker/`)

**The most important sentence:** "We don't compute the physics. NASA does the physics; we do the statistics."

MERRA-2 is a **reanalysis**: NASA's weather model combines satellite, station and balloon observations to produce a consistent record
for every ~55 km square of the world from 1980 to today. The physics (energy, water balance, etc.) is inside that model.

**Step by step (can be explained in 30 seconds):**
1. **Unit conversion:** Kelvin − 273.15 → °C · kg/m²/s × 86,400 → mm/day · kg/kg × 1,000 → g/kg · soil moisture × 100 → %.
2. **Province average:** the area-weighted mean of the squares inside the province (weighted by the cosine of latitude, because squares further north are smaller).
3. **Removing the season (anomaly):** how different is each month from the average of the same month in 1991–2020? (We compare July with other Julys, not with January.)
4. **Annual mean:** 46 years → 46 numbers. If a season is selected, only those months (winter = December + January + February).
5. **Mann-Kendall test:** compares every year with every later year: "is the later year higher?" If the count keeps rising, there is a trend. Result: a p-value (the chance it happened by luck).
6. **Sen's slope:** the **median** of the slopes between every pair of years → "how much per decade". Because it is a median, a single extreme year doesn't distort the result.
7. **95% confidence interval:** widened to account for consecutive years resembling each other (autocorrelation).

**When do we say "clear change"? (all at once)**
- The annual test has p < 0.01 and the 95% interval doesn't include zero,
- a second test on the monthly series (pre-whitened Mann-Kendall) also gives p < 0.01, same direction,
- dropping 5 years from the start and the end still gives p < 0.05, same direction,
- an independent source (NOAA stations, NASA GPCP precipitation) does not disagree.
If weaker, "possible change" (p < 0.05); if not even that, "no clear change".

**Likely follow-up questions:**
- **Why not linear regression?** Mann-Kendall makes no assumption about the data distribution and is robust to outliers; it is the standard method commonly used for climate trends (Mann 1945, Kendall 1975, Sen 1968).
- **Why annual rather than monthly?** In monthly data consecutive months resemble each other; a plain monthly test gave 17–31% false alarms. With annual means it dropped to ~5% (our own simulation).
- **How do you know it's physically right?** We tested the data against 6 known physical phenomena, and all 6 held: global precipitation = evaporation (difference 0.02%) · humidity rises ~7%/°C with warming (Clausius-Clapeyron, we measured 7.3%) · the Arctic warms 2.7× faster than the world · after the 1991 Pinatubo eruption, haze peaked in 1992 and temperature dropped · the warmest El Niño winters are 2015, 2023, 1997 · snow decreases where it warms.
- **Testing hundreds of places on the map at once?** There is a Benjamini-Hochberg (FDR) correction; false alarms stay below 5.5%.
- **Did you pick the rules after seeing the results?** No, the decision rules were fixed in PLAN.md before seeing the real data.

**If you're asked something you don't know:** saying "I can't say for sure, but it's explained step by step on the site under ⓘ → Method" is better than guessing.

## Regenerating the PDFs

With the app running at `localhost:5174` (with real data) and the presentation at `localhost:5180`, in the `presentation` folder:

```bash
npm run shots
```

```bash
npm run pdf
```

`shots` takes current screenshots of the app (world, the Diyarbakır card, time travel 1992 and 2024),
`pdf` produces the two PDFs. If something changes in the app, running both in order is enough.
